# Quiz Strategy: True Statement vs. Right Answer

## The Core Lesson

When a quiz question has a specific anchor word (e.g., *"turnaround-time"*, *"automation"*, *"architecture model"*), eliminate any answer that is **true but answers a different question**.

## Why Wrong Answers Are Tricky

The wrong answers in this course are almost never false — they are just answers to the question *next door*. The distractor is designed to catch people who know the material generally but don't read the question precisely.

### Example

**Question asks about:** Turnaround-time expectations

| Answer | Correct? | Why |
|--------|----------|-----|
| "Shorter turnaround times are expected for delivering solutions" | **YES** | Directly answers the specific question |
| "A wider range of tools and systems are available" | No | **True statement** — tool variety has increased. But it answers a question about *ecosystem breadth*, not turnaround time |

## The Rule

> **True statement ≠ Right answer.** The answer must match the *specific concept being tested*, not just be accurate about the field.
